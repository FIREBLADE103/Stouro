You are standing in what was once the centre of Roman public life: the Roman Forum.

The valley began as low, marshy ground. After it was drained and developed, it became a gathering place for commerce, religion and politics. Over the centuries, temples, public buildings and grand basilicas crowded around its streets and open spaces.

Here, Romans came to conduct business, attend religious ceremonies, hear speeches and take part in the political life of the city. The Forum was not one single building, but a constantly changing collection of places where public life unfolded.

Look for the remains of temples and triumphal monuments. The Arch of Septimius Severus, built in AD 203, celebrated the emperor's victories. Nearby stood buildings associated with Rome's religious traditions and its government.

The Forum changed as Rome changed. Emperors added monuments, old buildings were adapted, and the centre of power gradually shifted. After antiquity, much of the area fell into ruin and became buried beneath later layers of the city.

Today, the surviving columns and foundations mark the heart of a city that once shaped much of the Mediterranean world.
