Look at the Colosseum's enormous arches. When this amphitheatre opened under Emperor Titus in the year eighty, it became a stage for gladiatorial contests and hunts involving wild animals.
But the building itself was part of the spectacle. Its carefully designed entrances and passageways helped crowds reach their seats, while seating arrangements reflected the strict social hierarchy of Roman society.
Beneath the arena, hidden machinery allowed people, animals and scenery to appear through trapdoors. The underground systems were developed and altered over time, making the building far more than a simple arena.
Look at the missing sections of the outer walls. Over centuries, earthquakes, decay and the removal of building materials changed the monument dramatically.
What survives is still extraordinary: a glimpse of how Rome combined engineering, public entertainment and imperial power.
