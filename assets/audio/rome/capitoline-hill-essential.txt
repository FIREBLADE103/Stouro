Welcome to Capitoline Hill, one of the most sacred and politically important places in ancient Rome.

While the nearby Palatine became associated with emperors, the Capitoline was closely linked with Rome's gods and civic identity. At its summit once stood the great Temple of Jupiter Optimus Maximus, dedicated to Jupiter, Juno and Minerva.

The temple represented Rome's relationship with its gods and became a powerful symbol of the city's authority. Generals and leaders came to the Capitoline for important ceremonies, linking military success with divine favour.

The hill's role continued long after the ancient temples declined. During the Middle Ages, it became a centre of civic government. The Palazzo Senatorio still serves as Rome's city hall, preserving the hill's long connection with public authority.

Look around Piazza del Campidoglio, whose elegant design is associated with Michelangelo. The square's carefully arranged buildings create a very different atmosphere from the ancient city below.

Capitoline Hill brings together two sides of Rome: its ancient religious traditions and its continuing role as a centre of government and civic life.
