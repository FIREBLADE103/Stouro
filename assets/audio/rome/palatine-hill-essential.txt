Look up towards Palatine Hill, one of the most important places in the story of ancient Rome.

According to Roman legend, this was where Romulus founded the city in 753 BC. Archaeology tells a different, but equally fascinating, story: people were living on this hill long before Rome became a great power.

During the Republic, wealthy Romans built elegant houses here. Then Emperor Augustus chose the Palatine for his own residence, connecting the hill with imperial authority. Later emperors expanded it into a vast complex of palaces, courtyards and gardens.

The name of the hill became so closely associated with emperors that it gave us the modern word “palace”.

As you look across the ruins, imagine the contrast between the crowded streets below and the world of privilege above. From here, Rome's rulers lived close to its political centre, surrounded by architecture designed to express their power.

Palatine Hill is more than a collection of ruins. It is a place where Rome's legendary beginnings, wealthy households and imperial ambitions meet.
