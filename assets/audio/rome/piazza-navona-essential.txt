Look along Piazza Navona's long, elegant shape. It may look like a typical Italian square, but its outline comes from an ancient stadium that once stood here.

Emperor Domitian built the stadium in AD 86 for athletic competitions. Unlike the chariot races at the Circus Maximus, these events included contests inspired by Greek sporting traditions. The shape of the track survived long after the stadium itself disappeared.

During the seventeenth century, the square became one of the great stages of Baroque Rome. Its centrepiece is Bernini's Fountain of the Four Rivers, completed in 1651. Four monumental figures represent the Nile, Ganges, Danube and Río de la Plata — rivers associated with the continents known to Europeans at the time.

Nearby stands the church of Sant'Agnese in Agone, part of the square's grand architectural setting.

Today, cafés, street performers and visitors fill the space. Beneath that lively atmosphere lies a much older story: an ancient sporting venue transformed into one of Rome's most celebrated public squares.
