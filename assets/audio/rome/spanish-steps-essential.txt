Look up the sweeping staircase rising from Piazza di Spagna towards the church of Trinità dei Monti. The Spanish Steps were built between 1723 and 1726, turning a steep slope into one of Rome's most graceful public spaces.

Their name comes from the Spanish presence around the piazza below, where Spain maintained an important diplomatic establishment. The church above, meanwhile, had strong French connections. The staircase helped link these two parts of the city.

The architect Francesco De Sanctis designed a series of ramps, landings and changing directions that make the climb feel more like a sequence of small spaces than one long flight of steps.

For centuries, the steps have been a place to pause, meet friends and enjoy the view. In spring, the upper staircase is traditionally decorated with flowering azaleas, adding colour to the pale stone.

The Spanish Steps are not an ancient Roman monument. They belong to a later chapter of the city's history, when architecture was used to create elegant public spaces. Their lasting appeal lies in that combination of design, movement and everyday Roman life.
