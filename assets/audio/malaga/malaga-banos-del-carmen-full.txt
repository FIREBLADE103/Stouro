Baños del Carmen is a historic seaside leisure complex on the coast east of Málaga’s centre. It opened in 1918, at a time when outdoor bathing, sunshine and recreation were becoming increasingly fashionable. The project was aimed especially at Málaga’s bourgeois residents and the growing number of foreign visitors who came to the coast.

The complex was conceived as more than a place to enter the sea. It developed facilities for a full day of leisure, including a landing stage, tennis courts, a football pitch, a restaurant, an aquarium and a large dance floor. These amenities reflected a broader change in attitudes toward the coast. Sea bathing had once often been associated with health treatments and more controlled bathing establishments. By the early twentieth century, outdoor recreation, sport and social entertainment were increasingly part of the experience.

Baños del Carmen belonged to a new kind of seaside destination. Visitors could bathe, eat, watch events, dance or spend time with friends in one place. The complex also reflected social divisions of its period: it was initially designed with the tastes and expectations of more affluent customers in mind. Its story is therefore part of the history of leisure, but also of who had access to particular forms of recreation.

The rise of mass tourism from the 1960s onward changed the wider coast. New facilities and changing expectations made older bathing establishments less competitive, while public access to beaches expanded through legal and social change. Baños del Carmen’s original format gradually became outdated, and many of its earlier facilities disappeared or fell into disrepair.

The site’s appeal today comes partly from the traces of its earlier life and partly from its location. The shoreline and views across Málaga Bay create a setting that feels different from the dense historic centre. It is a place where the city meets the sea at a slower pace, and where the remains of a leisure complex can prompt questions about how public spaces should be preserved and reused.

The complex has been the subject of rehabilitation plans and public discussion. Restoration is not simply a matter of making an old building look attractive again. It involves decisions about which features to conserve, how to make the site safe and accessible, and how to balance historic character with contemporary uses. Those choices determine whether a place remains part of everyday life or becomes only a memory.

Baños del Carmen captures a particular moment in the history of the Mediterranean coast: the shift toward outdoor bathing and leisure as modern social activities. Its tennis courts, dance floor and restaurant made it a destination in its own right, not merely a stretch of beach. The surviving setting invites us to imagine that earlier culture while recognising that the coastline and its uses continue to change.
