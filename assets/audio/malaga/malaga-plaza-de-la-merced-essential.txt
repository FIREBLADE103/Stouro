Look around Plaza de la Merced: this open square has been a meeting place in Málaga for generations. Cafés and apartment buildings frame the space, while the central monument gives the plaza a more formal focus.

The square is closely associated with Pablo Picasso, who was born in Málaga in 1881 in a building on its northern side. His birthplace is now a museum, making this everyday city square part of the story of one of the twentieth century’s most influential artists.

At the centre stands an obelisk dedicated to General José María de Torrijos and the companions executed with him in 1831 after an attempt to oppose the absolutist rule of Ferdinand VII. The monument connects the plaza to a turbulent chapter in Spanish political history.

The square has changed its appearance and role over time, but it remains a place where different layers of Málaga meet: local life, public memory and artistic heritage.

As you look across it, notice that this is not a museum space sealed off from the city. It is still a working public square, with people meeting, resting and passing through. That living atmosphere is part of what makes Plaza de la Merced worth pausing for.
