Look at the entrance and the mix of architectural details on Iglesia de Santiago. The church is one of Málaga’s oldest Christian places of worship, traditionally associated with the period after the city was captured by the Catholic Monarchs in 1487.

The building began as a church in the late fifteenth century, and its appearance changed over time. One of its most striking features is the tower, whose form reflects the transition between earlier Islamic architectural traditions and Christian religious building. This blending of styles is a reminder that architecture often develops through adaptation rather than a clean break.

The church is also connected with Pablo Picasso, who was baptised here in 1881, the year he was born in Málaga. That link makes the building part of the artist’s early biography as well as the city’s religious history.

Look closely at the façade and tower rather than expecting the whole building to belong to one architectural moment. Different periods have left their marks.

Iglesia de Santiago is still a church, not simply a monument. Its significance comes from the combination of worship, architectural history and personal connections that have accumulated here over centuries.
