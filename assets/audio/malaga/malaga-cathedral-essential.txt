Look up at the great unfinished-looking tower and broad stone front of Málaga Cathedral. The building is one of the city’s most recognisable landmarks, but its story is not quite as simple as its grand appearance suggests.

After the Christian conquest of Málaga in 1487, a cathedral was established on the site of the city’s former main mosque. The present building took shape over several centuries, with work beginning in the sixteenth century. Its architecture blends Renaissance planning with later Baroque details.

The cathedral is often called La Manquita, meaning “the one-armed lady”. The nickname refers to the missing second tower: the southern tower was never completed. Stories have circulated about why the money ran out, but the precise history of the funds is more complicated than the popular legend.

Inside, the vast height and layered decoration reflect the ambition of a city seeking to express its religious and civic importance. From outside, notice how the unfinished tower gives the whole building an unusual silhouette.

Málaga’s cathedral is impressive not because every plan was completed, but because centuries of changing priorities remain visible in the stone.
