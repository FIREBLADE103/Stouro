From this hilltop, look out over Málaga, the harbour and the curve of the bay. Gibralfaro Castle occupies a strategic position above the city, built to defend the Alcazaba and watch the approaches by land and sea.

The fortress took its main form in the fourteenth century under the Nasrid ruler Yusuf I, with further work associated with his son Muhammad V. It was connected to the Alcazaba below by a fortified passage known as the Coracha, creating a linked defensive system.

The long walls follow the ridge, using the shape of the hill to strengthen the position. From above, defenders could observe the city and coastline, while the steep slopes made an assault more difficult. The name Gibralfaro is commonly linked to Arabic words for hill and a term derived from the Greek for lighthouse, reflecting the site’s long association with watching the coast.

After the Christian conquest of Málaga in 1487, the castle continued to serve military purposes and later became a barracks and prison. Today, its walls and viewpoints make the strategic logic of the site easy to understand.

The view is beautiful, but it was also practical. This hill was chosen because it allowed people to see who was approaching—and to defend the city if necessary.
