Take in the open space around you. Plaza de la Constitución has been a central public square in Málaga for centuries, serving as a place for trade, civic events and political gatherings.

The square has carried different names over time. It was known as the Plaza Mayor and, in the Nasrid period, as the square of the four streets. It took the name Plaza de la Constitución in 1812, linking it to Spain’s liberal constitutional movement.

The city hall once stood here, along with other important civic institutions. That concentration of public buildings made the square a natural setting for announcements, ceremonies and demonstrations. Even after the municipal government moved elsewhere, the plaza retained its role as a central meeting place.

Notice how several streets feed into the square. It is not a closed courtyard but a node in the historic street network, connecting shopping streets and older neighbourhoods. That layout helps explain why people naturally pass through it.

Today, cafés and pedestrians give the square a relaxed atmosphere, but its history is closely tied to public life and political change. Plaza de la Constitución is not just a picturesque pause in the city; it is a place where Málaga has repeatedly gathered to see and be seen.
