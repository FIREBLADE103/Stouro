Look up at the pointed arches, towers and detailed stonework of the Church of the Sacred Heart. Compared with Málaga’s older churches, this building has a distinctly neo-Gothic appearance, drawing on the medieval Gothic style that became popular again in the nineteenth century.

The church was built in the early twentieth century for the Jesuits and designed by the architect Fernando Guerrero-Strachan. Its vertical lines and ornate façade were intended to create a strong visual impression in the historic centre.

Neo-Gothic architecture did not simply copy medieval buildings. It used recognisable forms such as pointed arches, pinnacles and elaborate tracery to express religious identity and a sense of continuity with the Christian past. Here, those forms appear in a city whose architecture also carries Islamic, Renaissance and Baroque influences.

Notice how the façade rises above the surrounding streets. The church makes itself visible through height and detail rather than through a large open forecourt.

The building shows that Málaga’s architectural history did not stop in the medieval or early modern period. New churches continued to be built, drawing on older styles to express modern religious and civic ambitions.
