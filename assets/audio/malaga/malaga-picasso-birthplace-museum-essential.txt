Pause outside the building associated with Pablo Picasso’s birth. Picasso was born in Málaga on 25 October 1881, in a home on Plaza de la Merced. The city is only the beginning of his story, but it gives an important glimpse of the family and surroundings that shaped his early years.

His father, José Ruiz y Blasco, was a painter and art teacher. Picasso encountered drawing and painting early, and his family later moved away from Málaga as his father’s work took them elsewhere. The young artist eventually developed a career that transformed modern art, but he never lost the basic biographical connection to his birthplace.

The museum presents objects and material connected with Picasso’s life, family and artistic world. It is not the same as the larger Picasso Museum elsewhere in the historic centre: the birthplace focuses particularly on the artist’s origins and personal context.

The most important thing to remember here is the contrast between the scale of the later reputation and the ordinary city setting where the story began. Picasso did not emerge fully formed as a revolutionary artist. He was a child in a family, learning to observe and draw.

Look back toward Plaza de la Merced. The square links his private beginnings to the public life of Málaga, and helps place the famous name within a real city and a real childhood.
