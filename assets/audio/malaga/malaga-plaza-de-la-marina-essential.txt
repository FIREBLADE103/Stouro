Stand in Plaza de la Marina and look toward the harbour and the streets leading into the historic centre. This square sits at an important meeting point between Málaga’s waterfront and its commercial heart.

The area changed considerably as the port and city were modernised. The square’s present form belongs to the transformation of the waterfront during the twentieth century, when road connections, public space and access to the harbour were reorganised.

The port has long been central to Málaga’s fortunes. Goods, people and ideas arrived by sea, connecting the city to Mediterranean trade and to wider networks. The waterfront has changed from working harbour to a mix of maritime, commercial and leisure uses, but the connection between city and sea remains strong.

From here, you can orient yourself: the historic centre lies inland, while the harbour opens out toward the bay. The square functions as a threshold between these two worlds.

It may look like a traffic and transport space at first, but it is also a useful place to understand Málaga’s geography. The city grew around its relationship with the sea, and Plaza de la Marina makes that relationship visible in the modern urban landscape.
