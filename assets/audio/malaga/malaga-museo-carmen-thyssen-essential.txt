This museum brings together Spanish painting, with a particular emphasis on nineteenth-century art and scenes of Andalusian life. It opened in Málaga in 2011 in the historic Palacio de Villalón, a building whose origins reach back to the sixteenth century.

The collection is associated with Carmen Thyssen-Bornemisza and includes works that show how artists pictured Spain during a period of social and cultural change. Landscapes, portraits and everyday scenes offer a view of the country that is different from the grand history paintings often associated with national museums.

Look at the building as well as the art. The palace has been adapted for a modern museum, bringing a historic structure into contemporary cultural life. In the centre of Málaga, this is a recurring theme: older buildings are given new roles while retaining traces of their earlier identity.

Nineteenth-century artists often portrayed regional customs, rural settings and local dress. These images can be beautiful, but they also reflect the choices and expectations of the artists and audiences who produced them. They are not neutral photographs of everyday life.

The museum offers a way to explore both art and the idea of Spain that artists helped create. It adds another layer to Málaga’s cultural landscape beyond the city’s better-known connection with Picasso.
