Look at the curved exterior of La Malagueta Bullring, one of Málaga’s most recognisable nineteenth-century buildings. The bullring opened in 1876 and was designed by Joaquín Rucoba, an architect whose work also shaped other parts of the city.

Its circular plan reflects the requirements of bullfighting, with an arena surrounded by tiers of seating. The building uses a historicist style with neo-Mudéjar influences, drawing on architectural forms associated with Spain’s Islamic past. That style became popular in the nineteenth century, when architects often adapted historical motifs for modern public buildings.

Bullfighting has long been a controversial part of Spanish cultural life. For some, it is associated with ritual, regional identity and performance; for others, the harm to animals makes it unacceptable. A responsible visit can recognise the building’s architectural and historical significance without assuming everyone shares the same view of the event.

The bullring also houses a museum that presents aspects of the tradition and its history. Look at the façade’s repeated arches and brickwork, which give the building a distinctive character.

La Malagueta is a reminder that monuments can carry several meanings at once: architectural achievement, public entertainment, local identity and an ongoing ethical debate.
