Look out across the water at Baños del Carmen, a historic seaside bathing and leisure complex east of central Málaga. It opened in 1918, when sea bathing and outdoor recreation were becoming increasingly popular among the city’s better-off residents and visiting tourists.

The complex offered much more than access to the sea. At different times it included a landing stage, tennis courts, a football pitch, a restaurant, an aquarium and a large dance floor. It was designed as a leisure destination where people could spend an entire day by the water.

Its history reflects a change in how people understood the coast. Earlier bathing establishments often controlled access and treated sea bathing as a specialised activity. Baños del Carmen belonged to a newer culture of outdoor recreation, sport and social life.

The rise of mass tourism and changes in public beach access altered the role of the complex during the second half of the twentieth century. Some of its original facilities disappeared, and the site has since faced calls for restoration.

Today, the place is valued both for its history and for its position overlooking Málaga Bay. It offers a glimpse of an earlier seaside culture, when the beach was becoming a stage for modern leisure and public life.
