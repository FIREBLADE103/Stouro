Look toward La Farola, Málaga’s historic lighthouse near the port entrance. A lighthouse is a practical structure, built to help ships find their way, but this one has also become a recognisable symbol of the city’s maritime identity.

The lighthouse was completed in the early nineteenth century and entered service in 1817. It has been altered and repaired over time, as lighthouses must be maintained to withstand weather and changing technology. Its continued presence reflects the importance of safe navigation into Málaga’s harbour.

The name La Farola is distinctive in Spanish: *farola* is a feminine word for a lamp or streetlight, and Málaga’s lighthouse is often referred to with the feminine article. It has become part of local identity, not just a piece of port equipment.

Look at the relationship between the lighthouse, the breakwater and the open sea. Its position was chosen for visibility and navigation, rather than for a grand view of the city. Yet from the waterfront it forms a strong part of Málaga’s harbour scene.

La Farola reminds us that ports depend on more than ships and quays. They also need signals, charts, maintenance and people who keep vessels safe as they approach the coast.
